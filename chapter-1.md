# Chapter 1: The Rainy Departure

The clock above the train station platform ticked toward 6:15 AM, its hands glowing faintly under the fluorescent lights. Sophie tightened the straps of her backpack, her fingers brushing the worn leather of her journal peeking out from the side pocket. She’d packed it last, a habit she’d developed during her travels with Daniel—always something to write in, a way to capture the moments that might otherwise slip away. The train was late, as it always was on Fridays, and the platform was crowded with commuters clutching coffee cups and newspapers, their faces etched with the same weary resignation she felt. 

Daniel had called her at 5:30 AM, his voice thick with sleep and urgency. *"Sophie, I’m sorry. There’s been a contamination in the lab. I have to stay and oversee the decontamination process. I’ll try to make it, but it might not be until late tonight."* She’d hung up without answering, the weight of his words settling in her chest. This wasn’t the first time he’d canceled plans, nor would it be the last. For months, his work had consumed him, leaving her to navigate their relationship alone. 

She glanced at Mark, who stood a few feet away, his tall frame leaning against a pillar. His dark hair was tousled from the drizzle, and his hands were shoved deep into the pockets of his olive-green jacket. He held two paper cups of coffee, their steam curling into the gray morning air. When he saw her approach, he offered one with a small, tentative smile. *"For you,"* he said, *"I figured you could use it."*

Sophie took the cup, the heat seeping into her chilled fingers. *"Thanks. I thought Daniel was supposed to bring some."*

*"He called me on the way here,"* Mark said, avoiding her gaze. *"Said he’d be late. I figured I’d pick you up and at least make the wait a little less miserable."*

She nodded, taking a sip of the coffee. It was strong, bitter, and exactly what she needed. The train screeched into the platform, its doors hissing open with a plume of steam and rainwater. Sophie hesitated, her eyes flicking between the train and Mark. Daniel’s absence had created a void, and Mark’s presence filled it in ways she hadn’t expected. She’d known him for years—Daniel’s best friend since university, the calm to Daniel’s storm. But this was different. This was just them, alone, with no distractions.

*"Actually,"* she said, stepping forward, *"I think I’ll wait here with you. If Daniel’s going to be late anyway, we might as well make the most of it."*

Mark’s eyes widened slightly, then softened into a genuine smile. *"Yeah? You sure?"*

*"Yeah."* She took another sip of coffee, the bitter warmth grounding her in the moment. *"Let’s see what this rainy departure has to teach us."*

They walked to a small café adjacent to the station, its windows fogged with condensation. Inside, the air was warm and smelled of freshly baked pastries. They sat at a corner table, the silence between them comfortable, familiar. Mark ordered a second coffee, and Sophie toyed with a croissant, her thoughts drifting to the last time she’d felt this way—unburdened, present. It had been months, maybe longer. Daniel’s work had always been demanding, but lately, it felt like he was disappearing into it, leaving her to navigate their life alone.

*"You know,"* Mark said suddenly, breaking the silence, *"Daniel’s been talking about this trip for weeks. He was really excited about it. I think he just... forgot how to make time for it."*

Sophie looked up, her heart aching. *"He’s always busy. I don’t know if I can keep doing this."*

*"Then maybe you shouldn’t have to,"* Mark said quietly. *"Maybe it’s time to figure out what *you* want."*

The words hung between them, unspoken but heavy. Sophie felt a flicker of something—a spark of recognition, a quiet rebellion. She’d spent so long prioritizing Daniel’s needs, his ambitions, that she’d lost track of her own. But here, in this small café, with the rain tapping against the windows and Mark’s steady presence beside her, she felt a glimmer of something new. A possibility.

They spent the next hour talking—about nothing and everything. Mark told her about his carpentry projects, the way wood had a rhythm, a language all its own. Sophie shared stories from her bookstore, the eccentric customers and the quiet joy of curating shelves. When they finally stepped back onto the platform, the train had long since departed, and the station was nearly empty. The rain had stopped, leaving the air crisp and clean.

*"So,"* Mark said, gesturing to the empty tracks, *"what now?"*

Sophie smiled, a decision forming in her mind. *"We drive. To Devon. Without Daniel."*

Mark’s eyebrows shot up. *"Are you sure? I mean, we don’t even have a reservation—"*

*"Then we’ll find somewhere,"* Sophie said, cutting him off. *"Impromptu camping trip. Adventure."*

He hesitated, then grinned. *"Alright. Let’s do it."*

As they walked to Mark’s car, the sky began to clear, patches of blue peeking through the clouds. Sophie felt a strange sense of liberation, as if she’d shed a layer of expectation. For the first time in months, she wasn’t waiting for someone else—she was making her own choices, forging her own path. The road ahead was uncertain, but it was *hers*.
