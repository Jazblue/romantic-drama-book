# Chapter 1: The Rainy Departure

Sophie adjusted the straps of her backpack as she stood at the train station, watching the rain streak down the windows of the platform. The 6:15 AM train to Devon was late again, and she could feel the familiar flutter of excitement mixed with nervousness in her chest.

This trip had been Daniel's idea - a cheap camping weekend to reconnect before their third anniversary. Sophie had been skeptical at first, but after six months of Daniel's increasing absences due to work, she'd agreed, hoping the forced proximity might help them remember why they'd fallen in love.

Mark waited under the shelter of the station awning, his tall frame hunched against the drizzle. He held two paper cups of coffee, steam curling into the gray morning air. When he saw Sophie approach, he offered her one with a small, tentative smile.

"Thanks for coming," he said, his voice warm despite the chill. "I wasn't sure you'd actually show up."

Sophie took the cup, feeling the heat seep into her chilled fingers. "I said I would." She glanced at her watch. "Daniel's running late again, isn't he?"

Mark nodded, taking a sip of his coffee. "Called about twenty minutes ago. Said there was an emergency at the lab. Something about a sample contamination. He'll try to make it, but... well, you know how it is."

The words hung between them - not accusatory, just weary acceptance. Sophie had heard this story before. The important samples. The uncontrollable variables. The work that always came first.

As if on cue, the train screeched into the platform, its doors hissing open with a plume of steam and rainwater. Sophie looked at Mark, then at the train doors, and made a split-second decision.

"Actually," she said, stepping forward, "I think I'll wait here with you. If Daniel's going to be late anyway, we might as well make the most of it."

Mark's eyes widened slightly, then softened into a genuine smile. "Yeah? You sure?"

Sophie took another sip of coffee, the bitter warmth grounding her in the moment. "Yeah. Let's see what this rainy departure has to teach us."
