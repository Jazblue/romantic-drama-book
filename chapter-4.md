# Chapter 4: The Voice at the Window

Rain drummed against the tent walls, a relentless percussion that mirrored Sophie’s racing thoughts. She sat cross-legged on her sleeping bag, the journal open but untouched. Outside, the storm raged—wind howling, waves crashing against the rocks below. Inside, the lantern cast flickering shadows on Mark’s face as he repaired a torn tent flap. He worked silently, his hands sure and deliberate, but Sophie could sense the tension in his shoulders.

Her phone buzzed on the ground between them. *Daniel*. She hesitated, then picked it up. The message was brief: *"Lab emergency resolved. Will be at the campsite by morning. Love you."* Love. The word felt like a betrayal now, a lie repeated until it lost meaning. She stared at the screen, then slid the phone back to Mark.

"He’s coming," she said, her voice flat.

Mark glanced up, his expression unreadable. "When?"

"Tomorrow. Morning."

A muscle ticked in Mark’s jaw. He returned to mending the tent, but his movements were sharper, more deliberate. Sophie watched him, her chest tight. This was it—the moment she’d been both dreading and anticipating. The storm outside mirrored the one inside her, a tempest of unresolved questions: *What do I want? Who do I choose? Can I even choose at all?*

Later, as the rain eased to a drizzle, Mark built a small fire using dry twigs from a hidden stash. The flames sputtered to life, casting a warm glow over the damp clearing. Sophie wrapped herself in a blanket, her eyes fixed on the flickering light. Mark handed her a cup of instant cocoa, the marshmallows melting into the hot liquid. She took a sip, the sweetness sharp on her tongue.

"You don’t have to tell me anything," Mark said finally, breaking the silence. "But whatever you decide… I’ll respect it."

Sophie’s gaze snapped to his. "Decide? What’s there to decide?"

"Us," he said quietly. "You and me. This… whatever this is."

The word *us* hung in the air, heavy and undeniable. Sophie’s breath hitched. She’d known this moment was coming, but now that it was here, she felt unprepared. She opened her mouth to speak, then closed it, searching for the right words. How did she explain the chaos in her heart? The way Mark made her feel both safe and terrified?

"I don’t know," she whispered. "I don’t know what this is. I just… I don’t want to lose you either."

Mark’s eyes softened. He reached out, brushing a strand of hair from her face. "Then don’t. Whatever happens with Daniel… we’ll figure it out together."

Together. The word was a lifeline, but Sophie hesitated. Could she truly have both? Or was this just another illusion, like the fire that flickered wildly in the wind?

A crack of thunder shook the clearing. Sophie jumped, spilling cocoa on her blanket. Mark laughed, a warm sound that eased the tension. He grabbed a dry cloth, wiping her hand. Their fingers brushed, and Sophie felt that same unfamiliar warmth spread through her—a spark she both craved and feared.

"Stay with me," she blurted, the words escaping before she could stop them. "Tonight. Just… stay."

Mark froze, then nodded once. He kicked off his boots and slid into his sleeping bag, leaving a careful space between them. Sophie lay awake long after the fire died, listening to the rhythm of his breathing. For the first time in months, she didn’t reach for her phone. She didn’t think about Daniel or the lab or the future. She just existed—in the dark, in the storm, with Mark’s quiet presence beside her.
