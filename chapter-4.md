# Chapter 4: The Voice at the Window

Rain hammered against the glass of Elena's office building. The street below was bustling as usual, people hurrying to meetings, to lunch, to wherever their obligations took them. Elena sat behind her desk, fingers hovering over the keyboard, waiting.

Her phone buzzed against the edge of her desk. It was Daniel's number. He was calling from the lab, from the emergency room where he'd been working through the night.

"Sophie's there," Daniel said, his voice strained. "She called earlier. She's here now. The emergency's over."

Elena felt the sudden, sharp pain of jealousy. Who? What emergency? What did that mean?

She could hear the phone receiver being set down on the hook. The sound of that metal click always seemed to carry a certain weight — promise, possibility, change.

Yet he remained silent for a heartbeat, and when he spoke again, it was with a peculiar calm. "Look," he said quietly, "I've been having a lotOTD: The story I've been working on... I've finished it. Sophie read it last night. I think it's time we should let her decide what she wants to do. I've already submitted it to my editor. If she wants to work on her own project, that's her decision."

Silence stretched between them for what felt like an eternity. Elena's heart hammered against her ribs. This was her moment of truth — would she finally step into her own identity, or would she continue living in Daniel's shadow?

She knew what she wanted. She knew what she needed. She knew that the manuscript she was working on was her own creation, her own voice. She was ready to take ownership.

"Yes," she said, her voice steady. "Let's talk about it tomorrow. First thing."

His exhale was audible from the other end of the phone. "Good. See you then."

The call ended with a simple "okay." Elena hung up, the receiver clinking against the phone base. And in that moment of simple, uncomplicated contact, she understood that she didn't need to be Danielle anymore. She could be Elena.

She smiled softly at herself in the mirror behind her desk. Elena — not Daniel — was typing a new email to her editor.
