# Chapter 3: The Gentle Reckoning

The morning sun crept over the horizon, painting the sky in hues of amber and gold. Sophie sat on a weathered log by the fire pit, her journal open on her lap. The campsite was quiet except for the distant cry of seagulls and the rustle of leaves in the breeze. Mark had gone for an early hike, leaving her alone with her thoughts—a luxury she rarely allowed herself. 

She flipped through the pages of her journal, her pen hovering over a blank sheet. Memories flooded back: Daniel’s hands stained with ink as he sketched blueprints, his laugh echoing in their cramped apartment. Those days felt like a different lifetime, overshadowed by late-night lab calls and missed dinners. *When had passion become obligation?*

A twig snapped nearby. Sophie looked up to see Mark emerging from the hedge, his hair damp with dew, a wildflower tucked behind his ear. He held out a smooth, heart-shaped stone. "Found this by the cove," he said, his voice low. "Thought it might be good for skipping."

"Or for pressing," Sophie said, tucking it between the pages of her journal. Their fingers brushed, and she felt an unfamiliar warmth spread through her. She quickly looked away, focusing on the stone’s ridges. 

"You okay?" Mark asked, sitting beside her. "You’ve been quiet since last night."

Sophie hesitated. How did she explain the storm in her chest? The way Mark’s steady presence made her question everything? She closed her journal, tracing the stone’s edge. "I’ve been thinking… about Daniel. About us. I don’t know what I want anymore."

Mark nodded, not pushing. "Take your time. Whatever it is, it’s okay."

The fire crackled, sending sparks into the air. Sophie leaned back, watching the clouds roll in. A storm was coming—a literal one, and perhaps a metaphorical one too. She thought of Daniel’s text from last night: *"Still at the lab. Love you."* Love. The word felt hollow now, a placeholder for absence.

"Do you ever regret things?" she asked suddenly. "Choices you made?"

"Every day," Mark said without hesitation. "But regret is just data. It tells you where you went wrong so you can go differently next time."

Sophie turned to him, startled by the simplicity of his wisdom. "You make it sound easy."

"It’s not," he said, grinning. "But it’s a choice. To learn or to dwell."

They fell into a comfortable silence, the kind that didn’t need filling. Sophie realized that with Mark, she didn’t have to perform or explain herself. There was a ease in his presence, a quiet acceptance that felt like coming home.

Later, as rain began to patter against the tent, they huddled inside, playing card games by lantern light. Sophie laughed as Mark deliberately lost a round of Crazy Eights, his eyes crinkling. For the first time in months, she didn’t reach for her phone to check for Daniel’s messages. Instead, she savored the moment—the smell of wet earth, the sound of rain on nylon, the warmth of Mark’s proximity.

That night, as Sophie lay in her sleeping bag, she wrote in her journal by the faint glow of her headlamp: *"I used to think love was about grand gestures. Now I see it’s in the small things—the way he listens, the way he doesn’t rush to fix everything. Maybe I’ve been waiting for the wrong person to save me."*
