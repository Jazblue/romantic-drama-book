The Tuesday Massage

Chapter Seven: The New Arrangement

The massage returned. But so did the silence. And the weight of keeping two lives in separate boxes began to show.

Tuesday came again. Chloe arrived at two. Mark opened the door. He did not smile. Not at first. He stood aside and let her in, and the flat felt different — smaller, quieter, like the space between them had expanded to fill the room.

"Do you still want the massage?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Even after...?"

"Especially after."

He nodded. He did not push. He did not ask about Paul. He led her to the sofa. He laid out the towel. He heated the oil. The ritual was the same, but the rhythm was different — slower, heavier, like a conversation where nobody wants to speak first. Chloe lay on her stomach. She closed her eyes. She waited for his hands.

They came. Warm. Oil. Pressure. The hip was still tight — not as tight as it had been in September, not as angry as it had been in November, but stubborn, holding onto the memory of the injury like a grudge. Mark worked it slowly. He did not rush. He did not wander. He stayed on the muscle, on the knot, on the place where the tension lived. Chloe felt the familiar release — the softening, the melting, the moment where the body gives up what it has been holding.

But it was not enough. Not anymore. She wanted the extras. She wanted the wandering hand. She wanted the oil on her skin and his fingers sliding inside her knickers and the slow, deliberate exploration that had nothing to do with the hip. But she did not ask. She lay there. She let him work. She thought about Paul. She thought about the flat she shared with him. She thought about the life she was supposed to be living.

He finished. He wiped the oil away. He covered her with the towel. He sat back on his heels. "Done," he said.

She turned over. She sat up. She looked at him. He was wearing his usual — t-shirt, joggers, socks. He looked tired. He looked good. He looked like a man who had been waiting for something that was not coming.

"Can we..." She paused. She bit her lip. "Can we not stop there?"

Mark's eyes flicked to hers. "What do you want?"

"I want what we had. Before. The extras. The... the touching."

"Chloe."

"I know. Paul. I know. But I can't stop thinking about it. I can't stop thinking about your hands. About your mouth. About..." She stopped. She could not finish.

He did not answer immediately. He looked at her — really looked at her — like he was trying to decide whether she was worth the trouble, whether the risk was worth the reward, whether he could survive another round of this without breaking. Then he stood up. He walked to the kitchen. She thought he was leaving. She thought he was ending it. She felt her chest tighten.

But he came back. He was carrying a blanket. He handed it to her. "Cover yourself," he said. "We're not doing the massage. We're just... talking. Or not talking. Whatever you want."

She took the blanket. She wrapped it around herself. She sat on the sofa. He sat beside her. He did not touch her. Not at first. They just sat there, the rain on the window, the clock on the wall, the silence thick between them.

"I can't be your secret," he said, finally.

"I know."

"And I can't share you. Not really. Not the way Paul does."

"I know that too."

"So what are we doing?"

She did not answer. She stared at her hands. She thought about the choice she had been avoiding. She thought about Paul — safe, steady, good. She thought about Mark — complicated, desired, dangerous. She thought about the life she wanted. She thought about the life she had.

"I don't know," she said.

"That's not good enough."

"I know."

"Then figure it out."

He stood up. He walked to the window. He looked out at the rain. He did not look at her. She sat on the sofa, the blanket around her shoulders, the oil still faint on her skin, the hip still humming from the massage, and she thought: *He's right.*

She thought: *I have been avoiding this for too long.*

She thought: *I have to choose.*

But she did not choose. Not that day. She finished her tea. She put on her coat. She left. He did not walk her to the door. He did not kiss her. He just stood by the window and watched her go, and when the door closed, he leaned his forehead against the glass and thought: *I am so tired of waiting.*

Wednesday came. She went to work. She did her job. She smiled at the residents. She helped with lunch. She cleaned rooms. She thought about Mark. She thought about the massage. She thought about the way he had looked at her — not angry, not disappointed, just... tired. Like a man who had been waiting for something that was never going to come.

Thursday came. She did not go to Mark's. She stayed home. Paul was at work. The flat was quiet. She made tea. She sat by the window. She watched the rain. She thought about the choice. She thought about the conversation. She thought about the way he had said *figure it out*.

She thought: *I have to stop running.*

She thought: *But I don't know how.*

Friday came. Paul came home early. He had a surprise — a weekend away, this time to the coast, a B and B with a sea view, no work, no phone, no distractions. She said yes. She said yes because she thought distance would help. She said yes because she thought if she could just be away from Mark, just for a few days, maybe the confusion would clear.

It did not. The sea was loud. The room was small. Paul was affectionate. He held her hand. He kissed her neck. He tried to initiate sex on the balcony, under the stars, while the waves crashed below. She let him. She lay there. She closed her eyes. She pictured Mark — his hands on her hip, his mouth around him, the way he had looked at her on the sofa like she was the only person in the world.

Paul was not Mark.

Saturday morning, they walked on the beach. The wind was cold. The sand was wet. Paul held her hand. He talked about the future. He talked about moving in together properly, about a house with a garden, about children. Chloe listened. She nodded. She agreed. She let him plan. She did not tell him that she was not sure. She did not tell him that her mind was back in Mark's flat, on the massage table, in the space between his hands.

Saturday night, they made love again. It was gentle. It was careful. It was the kind of sex that people have when they are trying to make something work that is not working. She lay there and thought about Mark. She thought about the blowjob. She thought about the way he had held her afterwards and said *I want this*. She felt guilty. She felt dishonest. She felt like she was betraying Paul with a ghost and betraying Mark with a lie.

Sunday morning, they drove back. The car was quiet. Paul talked about the beach. He talked about the B and B. He talked about the future. Chloe nodded. She agreed. She let him talk. She did not tell him that she had spent the whole weekend thinking about someone else.

Monday came. Paul went to work. She made tea. She sat by the window. She looked at her phone. She thought about Mark. She thought about Tuesday. She thought about the massage.

She texted him. One word: "Sorry."

He replied instantly. "For what?"

"For running."

"You're not running. You're figuring it out."

"Does that make it better?"

"No. But it makes it honest."

She put the phone down. She stared at the rain. She thought: *I am not a good person.* She thought: *I am a woman who is trying to be honest in a situation that was never honest to begin with.*

She thought: *I have to stop running.*

She thought: *But I don't know where to stand.*
