# Chapter 2: Rogue Camping

The campsite was supposed to be pre-booked, but when they arrived at the Devon coastal caravan park, the cheerful woman at the reception desk delivered the news with a shrug: "Double-booked, love. Happens all the time in peak season."

"We have space," she continued, pointing to a muddy track leading behind the main facilities, "but it's not on the map. Bit rough round the edges - you'll need your own water supply, and the toilets are a five-minute walk. But it's yours if you want it."

Sophie exchanged a look with Mark. Daniel would have complained about the inconvenience, would have started scrolling through alternatives on his phone before they'd even finished speaking. But Mark just nodded, already assessing the situation with that practical calm she'd come to rely on.

"We'll take it," he said, and Sophie felt a small surge of gratitude for his adaptability.

They pitched their tent in a slight depression behind the main shower block, sheltered from the worst of the wind by a low hedge of blackthorn. The ground was uneven, littered with small stones and twigs, but they cleared a space and got the tent up with practiced efficiency. Sophie inflated their sleeping pads while Mark organized their gear inside the tent - food in one corner, water bottles in another, clothes neatly folded.

As they worked, Sophie noticed how Mark moved with a quiet competence, his hands sure and efficient. He didn't complain about the mud on his boots or the way the tent pegs kept slipping in the soft soil. When she struggled with a particularly stubborn guy line, he was there without being asked, his fingers quickly securing the knot.

"Thanks," she said, wiping her hands on her jeans.

"Anytime," he replied, not looking up from where he was securing the rain fly.

They spent the afternoon exploring the nearby coastline - a rugged stretch of cliffs and hidden coves that felt a world away from the managed neatness of the main campsite. Sophie found herself talking more than she had in months, pointing out interesting rock formations or seabird colonies, and Mark listened attentively, asking thoughtful questions that showed he was truly hearing her.

By evening, they'd established a rhythm. Sophie cooked a simple pasta dinner on their camping stove while Mark gathered firewood for their small, contained fire pit. They ate in companionable silence, watching the sky turn from blue to grey as the sun dipped behind the hills.

As darkness fell, they sat by the fire, sharing a bar of chocolate and telling stories - not the deep, revealing kind, but light, observational tales about weird customers Sophie had encountered at the bookstore or funny incidents from Mark's job as a carpenter. The fire crackled, the sea murmured in the distance, and for the first time in months, Sophie felt genuinely present, not just physically there but mentally and emotionally engaged.

When they retired to the tent, Sophie didn't immediately reach for her phone to check for messages from Daniel. Instead, she turned to Mark in the dim light filtering through the tent fabric and said, "Tell me something about your day that I don't already know."

And for the first time in a long while, they talked like they used to - easily, honestly, and with the sense that they were truly discovering each other all over again.
