# Chapter 2: Rogue Camping

The Devon coastline stretched out before them, a patchwork of rugged cliffs and secluded coves. Sophie rolled down the car window, letting the salty sea air rush in, tangling with Mark’s earthy scent of cedarwood and rain. The campsite had been a last-minute decision, born from a shared glance and an unspoken understanding. Now, as they pulled into the gravel parking lot, Sophie felt a flicker of uncertainty. The woman at the reception desk had been right—the site was rough, unpolished. But there was a freedom in that, a chance to shed the polished expectations of life.

"This way," Mark said, pointing to a narrow path behind the main shower block. He grabbed two heavy duffel bags from the trunk, his muscles bunching under his gray sweater. Sophie followed, her boots crunching on loose stones. The path wound downhill, revealing a small clearing sheltered by a gnarled hedge. The ground was uneven, littered with twigs and pebbles, but the view of the sea was worth it—a patch of turquoise visible between the branches.

"Let’s set up here," Mark said, dropping the bags. He unzipped a large tent sack, pulling out a bright blue pop-up tent. Sophie watched as his hands moved with practiced ease, snapping poles into place. She unrolled their sleeping pads, her fingers brushing against the stiff nylon. This was different from the camping trips she’d taken with Daniel, who’d always insisted on high-tech gear and precision planning. With Mark, there was a relaxed confidence, a willingness to adapt.

"Need help?" Sophie asked, offering a tent stake.

"Got it," Mark said, driving the stake into the soft earth. "But thanks. You can start organizing the food supplies. I packed some instant noodles, canned beans… and these," he grinned, pulling out a bag of marshmallows, "for later."

Sophie laughed, the sound foreign to her ears. When was the last time she’d laughed like this? She began unpacking the cooler, arranging cans and jars on a flat rock. The sun dipped lower, casting long shadows across the clearing. Birds chirped in the hedge, and somewhere nearby, the distant crash of waves provided a rhythmic backdrop.

As dusk settled, they built a small fire pit using stones from the beach. Mark gathered driftwood, his hands blackening with soot as he arranged the logs. Sophie struck a match, watching flames lick at the kindling. The fire crackled, casting dancing shadows on the tent walls.

"Hungry?" Mark asked, handing her a skewered marshmallow.

"Starving," Sophie said, roasting it over the flames. The sugar caramelized, dripping gold onto the coals. She took a bite, the sweetness melting on her tongue. For the first time in months, she felt truly *present*, her senses alive to the crackle of fire, the salty tang of the sea, the warmth of Mark’s quiet companionship.

Later, as they sat cross-legged by the fire, Mark pulled out his journal—a leather-bound notebook filled with sketches of wood carvings. "Daniel told me you write too," he said, flipping to a page of intricate designs. "What do you write about?"

Sophie hesitated, then reached into her backpack, pulling out her own journal. Its pages were filled with short stories, vignettes about customers at the bookstore, the way light filtered through stained glass, the quiet moments between conversations. She handed it to Mark, her heart racing as he leafed through it.

"These are beautiful," he said softly, pausing at a passage about a woman who found solace in a secondhand bookshop. "You have a way of making the ordinary feel magical."

Sophie blushed, looking away. "It’s just… observations. Things that stick with me."

"That’s what art is," Mark said, closing the journal. "Capturing the moments that stick."

The fire popped, sending sparks into the twilight. Sophie glanced at Mark, his profile illuminated by the flames. There was a tenderness in his gaze, a quiet understanding she hadn’t expected. She opened her mouth to speak, then closed it, unsure of what to say. The night was perfect, fragile, and she didn’t want to break it with unspoken questions.

Instead, she reached for another marshmallow, and they sat in comfortable silence, listening to the sea.
